1

Watch the reference reel 30 sec

Play it twice. Notice three things: the hook lands in the first 2 seconds, the key point is always burned in as big on-screen text, and it ends with a clear "comment link" call-to-action. That's the whole formula.

2

Pick a script copy

Choose your niche below, tap any card, and hit Copy full script. The hook, timestamped lines, shot list and caption are all done — paste them into your notes. [Bracketed] bits are your slots; fill them with your own example or a paper from the research strip.

3

Set up your shot 1 min

Phone vertical (9:16), propped at eye level. Face a window so the light is on you, not behind you. Quiet room, phone on a stack of books or a cheap tripod. No fancy gear needed — the reference reel is shot on a phone.

4

Nail the hook first 2 sec

Record the hook line straight to camera with energy before anything else. This first sentence decides whether people keep watching. Do 3–4 takes and keep the punchiest one. No slow intro, no "hey guys" — open on the hook.

5

Film the body in short takes 5 min

Shoot one script beat per take — glance at the timestamped lines between takes. Fumble a word? Just re-record that beat. Add the demo or personal example where the script says to. Keep your pace fast; dead air kills retention.

6

Add captions + the big text edit

In CapCut / Reels editor: auto-caption the whole thing (most watch on mute), then drop the key finding as large text on screen exactly like the reel. Cut the gaps between takes so it's punchy. Optional: a trending low-volume audio underneath.

7

Post with the CTA go live

Paste the card's caption + hashtags. End your video and caption with the same move as the reel: "comment 'link' and I'll send it" or "follow for one study a week." Cite the paper in your bio. Post, reply to every comment in the first hour.
